Section 1

What Are Secondary Credit Bureaus?

Most people have heard of Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. But the system that evaluates your financial life is much bigger than three companies.

Secondary credit bureaus collect and report specialized data that the major bureaus don't always capture — and financial institutions, landlords, employers, and insurers are using that data to make decisions about you right now.

If you've been focused only on your Big 3 reports, you may be missing a significant part of your own financial story. This lesson is about closing that gap.

Section 2

Innovis

Innovis operates like a fourth major credit bureau. It collects much of the same data as Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion — payment history, account balances, public records — but most people have never heard of it.

That's the problem. Lenders do use Innovis data when making credit decisions, and errors in your Innovis file carry the same weight as errors on your Big 3 reports. If something is wrong there, it can cost you an approval or a favorable rate without you ever knowing why.

Your Innovis report may include:

  • Credit accounts and payment history
  • Personal identifying information
  • Public records
  • Collection accounts

You are entitled to a free Innovis report and have the right to dispute anything inaccurate.

Section 3

PRBC — Payment Reporting Builds Credit

Here's a reality many people never consider: you may have paid rent on time for years, covered every utility bill, and kept your phone on — and none of it shows up on your credit report.

The three major bureaus weren't designed to capture alternative payment data. PRBC exists to change that. It tracks recurring payments that traditional credit bureaus ignore, giving people with thin or limited credit files a way to demonstrate financial responsibility through behavior that already exists.

Your PRBC report may include:

  • Rent payment history
  • Utility payment history
  • Phone and subscription payments
  • Self-reported payment records

If you've been financially responsible but your credit profile doesn't reflect it, this is one of the reasons why. Knowing about PRBC is the first step toward using it.

Section 4

ChexSystems

ChexSystems doesn't track credit — it tracks your banking behavior. Most banks and credit unions pull a ChexSystems report before approving a new checking or savings account.

If you've ever had a bank account closed due to overdrafts, unpaid fees, or suspected fraud, that record may still be in your ChexSystems file. And unlike credit inquiries that fade over time, negative ChexSystems records can prevent you from opening a bank account anywhere for up to five years.

No bank account creates its own set of problems — it makes it harder to receive direct deposit, build savings, and access mainstream financial products.

Your ChexSystems report may include:

  • Unpaid overdraft fees
  • Forced account closures
  • Suspected fraudulent activity
  • Returned check history

You are entitled to a free ChexSystems report every 12 months and have the right to dispute inaccurate entries.

Section 5

LexisNexis Risk Solutions

LexisNexis compiles one of the most detailed personal data profiles in existence. It pulls from public records, court filings, property ownership history, and hundreds of other data sources — and this information is actively used by insurance companies, employers, and financial institutions to evaluate risk.

Most people don't know their LexisNexis file exists. But others are using it to make decisions about them every day.

Your LexisNexis report may include:

  • Previous addresses and name variations
  • Property ownership history
  • Court and bankruptcy records
  • Professional license information
  • Insurance claims history

You have the right to request and review your LexisNexis personal report. If there are inaccuracies, they can affect your insurance rates, background checks, and more.

Section 6

Clarity Services

Clarity Services focuses on consumers who use non-traditional lending products — payday loans, installment loans, rent-to-own arrangements. It serves lenders who operate outside the standard credit system and work with people who may have been declined elsewhere.

If you've used any of these products, your activity is likely being tracked in a Clarity file you've probably never seen. Understanding this is important because Clarity data can influence whether you're approved and what terms you're offered — even when traditional bureaus are less relevant.

Your Clarity report may include:

  • Payday loan history
  • Installment loan activity
  • Rent-to-own account history
  • Short-term lending patterns

Section 7

Why This Matters to You

When most people work on their credit, they check three reports. But the system that evaluates your financial life includes far more than three bureaus — and every one of them can contain errors that cost you money, opportunities, and access.

The reason most people don't know about these bureaus isn't because they're hidden. It's because no one teaches this. That's exactly why this lesson exists.

Understanding secondary bureaus means understanding the full scope of your financial profile. It means knowing where to look, what you're entitled to see, and what your options are when something isn't right.

As you continue through NDCG, you'll learn how to request these reports, review them properly, and take action when something doesn't belong.
